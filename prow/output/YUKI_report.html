Open But Unreachable?

When the quarterback throws, does it matter how long a receiver has been open, or only whether he is open at that instant? We measure it with PROW, the Pre-Release Open Window: the number of seconds a receiver has been continuously at least 3 yards from every defender, counted back from the release.

This is a film-review list, not a ranking: the intervals overlap. It covers 27 QBs with at least 150 throws, whose rates range from 18% to 31%.

Method
  • Data: 7,221 targeted throws in 122 games (complete, incomplete and intercepted passes with a snap and a release in the tracking). Sacks and scrambles are excluded because there is no release to measure from.
  • Route runners come from PFF pff_role = "Pass Route". The targeted receiver is parsed from the official play-by-play text and matched only against that play's route runners.
  • PROW uses only tracking frames between the snap and the release, at 10 Hz (checked against the timestamps). Pass outcomes are used only to evaluate the metric.
  • Uncertainty: confidence intervals resample whole games. The logistic regression uses standard errors clustered by game and controls for separation at release, depth and time to throw.
  • Checks: separation, PROW and time to throw were recomputed with an independent brute-force loop on random receivers, and all match exactly.
Limits
  • The nearest defender is not necessarily the defender assigned to that receiver.
  • Being geometrically open is not the same as being reachable: we do not model the QB's progression, his sight lines, the pass rush or the throwing lane.
  • These are associations, not causal effects. The data cover 8 weeks of a single season.
